Design notes

Four eyes,
one mind.Design notes · MII / 02

mii is not a device you look at. It is four quiet columns at the corners of a room, a hub you never see, and light that falls on the walls you already own. The columns only sense; the hub alone thinks; the display is whatever light engine the room can carry today — and something better tomorrow.

Every part is replaceable without touching the others. The sensing core is fixed for a decade. The display layer is changed like a lens. The hub runs the same RockstarOS as a phone with an eSIM, so the room continues the conversation you started on the train.

01 · ContextRGB fisheye, 160–180°, 60 fps. Room, furniture, tools, people as meaning.
02 · TrackingGlobal-shutter NIR, 1920 × 1280, 120 fps, 100° lens, 850 nm filter. Head region sent at full resolution; body at 2 × 2 binning.

06 · ShutterMechanical lens shutter. Closed, the room is sensed by radar only.

What a node does not do

It does not think. It does not store. It does not connect to anything but its hub. Four nodes are one instrument; the hub is the only mind in the room.

Design notes

From one
to four.Design notes · MII / 03

A room begins with one column and grows. Every column added is a new point of view the hub fuses immediately; nothing is replaced, nothing is reconfigured. What changes is how much of you the room can see — and therefore what it can do.

OneWhere you are, how you move, where your head is. Eve’s voice and face. Motion parallax already works — the world stays inside the wall.
TwoDiagonal pair, longest baseline. True depth by triangulation; eyes located to ±10 mm. Objects may stand on the floor and lean 30 cm out of the wall.
ThreeTurn your back and tracking holds. The renderer keeps your own shadow off the thing you are looking at.
FourThe whole body, all round. Guides projected onto your own limbs; the tip of a shinai at 10 m/s; a life-size partner in another city standing where you stand.
EightRooms beyond six metres, several people, a factory floor. Same hub family, same calibration, same API.

Every node is an equal. There is no master column, only the hub, which keeps the clocks, schedules the radar, chooses the two to four best views for each joint, and reports a single word to the software — presence, pose2d, pose3d, robust, full — so that a game knows what the room can promise today.

Take a column away and the room degrades gracefully: the picture never stops, the quality word changes, Eve says so. Add one and calibration takes a minute: hold the target, walk once round the room, done.

Optics · what you see

No glasses. No screen. A wall that is no longer a wall.

Generation one draws the world the way a window would show it. Four columns know where your eyes are to a centimetre and where they will be in thirty milliseconds. The light engine redraws the wall and the floor from exactly that point, so the room beyond the wall keeps its depth as you walk, and a tetrahedron on the floor stays put when you circle it. For one person it is immersion; for a second person it is a clever painting — which is why remote play puts one player in each room.

Generation two adds a real aerial image — thirty to fifty centimetres of light that exists in the middle of the room, formed by retro-reflective optics in the heads of the columns and shared by everyone present. The columns do not change. Only the light does.
